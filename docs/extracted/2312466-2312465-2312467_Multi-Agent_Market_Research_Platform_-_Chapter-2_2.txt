Background and Literature Review
A structured literature review establishes the foundation for modern software engineer-
ing research and development. Comparative analysis identifies practical shortcomings
across manual survey platforms and clarifies why an automated architecture is neces-
sary. Software benchmarking positions ongoing development within the wider computing
discipline, spotlighting unresolved operational bottlenecks and setting concrete techni-
cal boundaries for engineering choices. This chapter systematically evaluates relevant
academic publications alongside commercial mobile survey systems to substantiate the
design of the proposed platform [4].
A clear roadmap will be followed throughout this chapter. In Section 2.1, capabilities
and limitations of existing survey platforms will be evaluated. Practical field usage of a
future platform will be examined in Section 2.2, from which the formal problem state-
ment will be formulated in Section 2.3. The proposed market research platform will be
described in Section 2.4, followed by a justification of the selected software process model
in Section 2.5.
2.1 Existing System
Software engineers conduct rigorous comparative evaluations of existing commercial
platforms to ground new system development. Systematic product benchmarking identi-
fies standard operational features across deployed survey tools while exposing unaddressed
technical shortcomings in field workflows. Software benchmarking positions ongoing de-
velopment within the wider computing discipline, spotlighting unresolved operational
bottlenecks and setting concrete technical boundaries for engineering choices. Investigat-
ing production software architectures prevents development teams from repeating known
design flaws and reinventing established engineering mechanisms. Understanding these
functional boundaries establishes an empirical justification for building specialized soft-
ware architectures. This section systematically evaluates ten widely used data collection
platforms, analyzing offline SQLite storage, spatial billboard registries, and multi-agent
competitive workflows [4], [5].
Ten systems are reviewed in this section: Qualtrics, SurveyMonkey, ODK, KoboTool-
box, Fulcrum, SurveyCTO, CommCare, FastField, GeoPoll, and QuickTapSurvey. Se-
lected as established market leaders and open-source standards, these applications en-
able survey authoring and offline field collection. Despite their individual strengths, they
consistently lack multi-agent reasoning, automated billboard evidence verification, and

2.2 Future System Usage Analysis
Market research systems will shift toward autonomous data collection and spatial
intelligence across the advertising industry. Field survey platforms will incorporate co-
operative artificial intelligence agents to coordinate campaign planning and competitor
monitoring. Rather than relying on static questionnaires, mobile applications will run
on-device computer vision models to evaluate billboard viewing angles, physical condi-
tion, and ambient lighting in real time. Smart synchronization protocols will store survey
submissions locally on mobile hardware and upload batches as wireless networks emerge.
Multi-agent software pipelines will automatically cross-reference street photographs with
secondary web data, uncovering market trends and flagging inconsistent responses for
human review. Brand strategists will consult conversational dashboards to query survey
metrics and trace claims directly to physical site evidence. Through these continuous
advancements, modern platforms will transform out-of-home advertising measurement
into a verifiable, tamper-resistant discipline.

2.3 Problem Statement / Limitations
Outdoor advertising research currently relies on manual field auditing routines across
metropolitan media markets. Survey organizations deploy field enumerators equipped
with consumer smartphone applications to inspect roadside billboard structures. Field
auditors record descriptive questionnaire responses, photograph commercial advertising
displays, and note geographic coordinates across daily audit routes. Central analysts
manually pair detached photographs with corresponding survey records before compiling
advertiser campaign reports. Commercial brand managers depend on these audits to
verify physical display condition, monitor competitor spending, and confirm contractual
campaign delivery. The traditional auditing process exhibits severe operational limita-
tions for large-scale media verification. Current field practices lack offline-first SQLite
synchronization queues for remote survey areas, automated computer vision scoring for
billboard visibility, and coordinated multi-agent workflows for competitive market intel-
ligence.
The proposed system operates under defined practical constraints and concrete en-
gineering limitations throughout development. The engineering team confines physical
testing to regional commercial districts within Islamabad before attempting multi-city au-
dits. The development team manages this timeline constraint by conducting structured
field trials across accessible commercial hubs. Inherent variations across mobile smart-
phone hardware introduce data collection hurdles, as entry-level Android devices produce
uneven photographic resolution and sensor noise. To resolve this hardware limitation, the
backend architecture applies contrast adjustments and normalization algorithms to stan-
dardize captured photographs before automated evaluation. Furthermore, commercial
outdoor rate cards remain largely proprietary and absent from open web databases. The
team mitigates this data scarcity by instructing automated web agents to extract public
media listings and comparing them against synthetic valuation models. The development
team consciously addresses these operational boundaries across development sprints to
ensure successful milestone delivery.
2.4 Proposed Solution
The proposed software architecture establishes an integrated mobile and cloud plat-
form to automate outdoor media verification across metropolitan commercial areas. Field
enumerators inspect roadside billboard structures using a native Android smartphone
application operating without cellular connectivity. The mobile application records high-
resolution photographs, timestamps, and geospatial coordinates within local SQLite database
tables before pushing data to central servers. Central FastAPI backend services ingest
incoming survey submissions and store relational campaign records within a PostgreSQL
database with PostGIS extensions. LangGraph multi-agent workflows search external

2.5 Software Process Model
A software process model provides a systematic engineering framework that directs
the lifecycle of modern digital systems from inception to release. This framework or-
ganizes complex engineering tasks into predictable phases, practical activities, and clear
team workflows. Development teams achieve superior coordination, dependable milestone
tracking, and high system reliability through such operational discipline. System char-
acteristics such as overall scope complexity, delivery deadlines, and requirement fluidity
determine the most suitable development methodology. This section outlines and defends
the software process model for the system [4].
The engineering group selects Agile Scrum because developing multi-agent software
demands rapid experimentation on raw media assets. Upcoming two-week sprints will
produce functional Flutter components and backend APIs, while bi-weekly demos will
guide the system toward timely milestone delivery.
2.5.1 Introduction
The development team adopts the Agile Scrum framework to direct system engineer-
ing across recurring cycles. This operational methodology divides complex engineering
goals into distinct two-week iterations, encouraging cross-functional teamwork and early
prototype evaluations. The framework suits the system because engineering autonomous
research agents, offline SQLite synchronization queues, and YOLO vision algorithms de-
mands ongoing practical trial. Software developers work across successive sprints to refine
FastAPI backend services, construct Flutter mobile interfaces, and optimize multi-agent
LangGraph workflows. Each development cycle begins with sprint planning, proceeds into
active programming, and concludes with functional code testing and supervisor demon-
strations. This rhythmic workflow helps developers resolve software defects promptly
and integrate updated survey requirements without delaying scheduled milestone deliv-
ery. Figure 2.1 illustrates the complete development workflow for the system [4].

2.5.2 Justification
The development team chooses the Agile Scrum methodology to manage engineer-
ing risks across multi-tier software modules. Building mobile survey clients, cloud spa-
tial databases, and autonomous web agents involves tight technical interdependencies.
Scrum divides these technical dependencies into manageable two-week engineering cy-
cles, enabling developers to discover database bottlenecks early and revise agent prompts
promptly. This iterative workflow allows the team to deliver stable software increments
while meeting all system research requirements.
2.5.3 Steps
The development team will structure engineering milestones across four distinct Scrum
iterations. Initial sprints will configure PostgreSQL schemas and local SQLite storage
queues. Subsequent cycles will produce LangGraph multi-agent orchestration pipelines,
computer vision quality filters, and central verification web consoles for research analysts.
Sprint 1: Core Architecture & Data Ingestion: The engineering group will
build foundational database tables and authentication APIs during the opening sprint.
Software developers will configure a cloud PostgreSQL instance with PostGIS geospatial
modules, and the team will write baseline FastAPI endpoints to ingest incoming survey
submissions from mobile clients.
Sprint 2: Mobile Client & Field Verification: Mobile engineers will construct
the Flutter data collection client during the second sprint. Field staff will photograph
roadside billboard structures and record coordinate markers through an Android client.
The mobile application will cache inspection logs within local SQLite database files when-

Sprint 3: Multi-Agent Intelligence & Vision Scoring: System developers will

during the third sprint. Multi-agent Python scripts will scrape external commercial

foliage obstruction, lighting glare, and angle distortion.
Sprint 4: Analytics Dashboard & System Verification: The development team

busy Islamabad commercial districts before final release.
