Chapter 1
Introduction
The out-of-home advertising industry reports $ 9.46 billion in revenue, yet market re-
searchers rely on fragmented data tools. Field workers record GPS coordinates, pho-
tographs, and surveys across separate applications. This disconnected approach demands
roughly 50 staff-hours per 100 locations for manual data reconciliation. Analysts struggle
to verify unmatched field evidence against digital campaign logs.
The proposed system provides a unified web and Android architecture to eliminate
these bottlenecks. Administrators structure campaigns through a central web portal.
Field staff capture observations via the Android application. A local SQLite database
secures offline submissions and forces incremental synchronization to PostgreSQL servers
when internet access returns.
The software deploys specialized artificial intelligence agents to automate analytical
workflows. Distinct modules coordinate research planning, secondary data extraction,
and cross-source verification. LangGraph-driven tools evaluate billboard image quality,
process media attachments, and calculate performance metrics. The automated pipeline
routes anomalous records directly to human reviewers for final validation.
Advertising agencies utilize this traceable workflow to publish reliable market intelli-
gence. The unified pipeline protects data provenance from street-level capture to execu-
tive reports. Research firms eliminate repetitive manual validation and scale operations
safely. The following sections describe the system purpose, system scope, development
objectives, intended market, and target users.
1.1 Product Purpose
The proposed system is a centralized web and Android workspace for out-of-home
advertising research. The unified platform organizes field surveys, GPS coordinates,
and media attachments inside a secure PostgreSQL database. Older disconnected tools
make organizations waste fifty staff-hours reconciling data for campaign locations. The
Android application resolves these specific administrative bottlenecks through SQLite-
based offline persistence and network synchronization. Specialized artificial intelligence
agents analyze billboard image quality, cross-reference competitor data, and compile per-
formance metrics. The automated workflow sends anomalous records directly to human
reviewers for final validation. The new architecture replaces manual data entry tasks with
verifiable audience measurement standards. Advertising agencies deploy this traceable

1.3 Objectives
The software development methodologies define objectives as specific, measurable,
achievable, relevant, and time-bound statements. These SMART statements translate
high-level system goals into concrete technical outcomes. The clear objectives guide de-
velopment priorities for the engineering team. The documented targets dictate proper
resource allocation throughout the software lifecycle. The system stakeholders use these
measurable statements to communicate expectations clearly. The finalized objectives pro-
vide a strict mathematical basis for system validation. The quality assurance evaluators
compare the final software deliverables against these exact original criteria [2].
The proposed system delivers twelve functional modules by the final system evalua-
tion. The User Management module establishes secure company workspaces and strict
device authorization protocols. The Campaign Management module connects specific
billboard advertisements to hierarchical market locations. The Field Data-Collection
module enables offline questionnaire responses through the custom Android application.
The Data Synchronization module pushes the SQLite records to the PostgreSQL server
upon internet reconnection. The automatic synchronization tests guarantee zero dupli-
cate submissions during these network transfers. The Attendance Verification module
validates field surveyor visits through exact GPS records and geofences. The Media
Processing module evaluates site photograph quality and preserves direct evidence links.
The Market Intelligence module gathers secondary out-of-home advertising metrics for
accurate competitor comparisons. The Agent Orchestration module manages the Lang-
Graph artificial intelligence agents for research planning and data extraction. The au-
tomatic AI verification pipeline detects inconsistent survey information instantly. The
routing algorithm directs uncertain field evidence directly to human reviewers. The Field
Performance module evaluates visit completion rates and audio pitch recordings. The
AI-Agent Analytics module presents complex campaign comparisons through interactive
dashboards. The final evaluation compares manual and multi-agent workflows through
strict completion time, accuracy, and failure recovery metrics.
The successful completion of these objectives will fulfill the core purpose of the
proposed system. The unified architecture will connect fragmented research activities
with traceable field evidence. The out-of-home advertising agencies will gain a common
workspace for reliable campaign management and market comparison. The field staff will
retain their survey submissions securely during sudden connectivity interruptions. The
robust synchronization protocols will prevent data loss after network reconnection. The

1.4 Intended Market of Product
The system serves more than one market segment. The target customers include
research companies, advertising agencies and businesses that study their own customers.
The following subsections explain the needs of each market and how the system supports
its research work.
Market Research Companies: The market research segment encompasses small
consultancies and massive enterprise firms. These organizations collect survey responses
and supervise field teams across multiple cities. The recent industry reports indicate
massive data quality problems across this sector. Poor data quality costs these orga-
nizations millions of dollars annually. The manual reconciliation process requires fifty
staff-hours for every one hundred campaign locations. The analysts struggle to keep
GPS coordinates, media files, and survey responses consistent throughout a study. The
proposed system will eliminate these exact administrative bottlenecks through secure
company workspaces and offline SQLite collection. A firm will keep campaign responses
and supporting evidence together automatically during fieldwork. The LangGraph agents
will inspect source links instantly. The automated verification pipeline will route uncer-
tain records to human reviewers. The field managers will monitor assignment completion
through interactive dashboards. The firm will spend significantly less time repeating
validation tasks. This streamlined workflow will help the firm manage research costs
effectively. The researchers will present findings to clients with undeniable evidence.
Advertising and Media Agencies: The advertising agencies plan massive out-of-
home campaigns for corporate clients. The out-of-home advertising sector generates over
nine billion dollars in annual revenue. The digital screens represent a massive portion of
that spending. These agencies need reliable metrics to justify these massive client invest-
ments. The current evidence arrives as disconnected photographs and paper survey files.
This fragmented separation makes client reporting incredibly difficult. The executives
require a reliable method to match field activity against the original campaign plan. The
PostgreSQL database will connect specific digital advertisements with hierarchical mar-
ket locations. An agency will utilize these database connections to validate field progress
accurately. The managers will view surveyor attendance through geofenced dashboards
before preparing the final campaign report. The direct evidence links will help the agency

ward specific evidence gaps. The automated synchronization will prevent field teams from
submitting duplicate records. The agencies will gain a massive competitive advantage

Consumer Brands and Retail Businesses: The retail businesses conduct inde-
pendent research to understand customer preferences. The target market includes local
single-outlet shops and massive national brands. These businesses require accurate an-
swers about competitor messaging and market differences. The internal staff members
combine web survey files with physical field observations manually. The executives strug-

data entry limits their ability to assess actual brand awareness. The proposed system

1.5 Intended Users of the Product
The proposed system supports six distinct user groups across the market research
lifecycle. Each professional group handles a different stage of the out-of-home advertising
workflow. The following subsections define the specific administrative responsibilities of
these users. The descriptions explain exactly how the new platform will assist each group.
Company Administrators: The company administrators control access to the cen-
tral workspace. These technical users manage role permissions across fragmented software
systems currently. The administrators struggle to secure data across disconnected field
applications. The administrators will utilize the User Management module to create se-
cure agency accounts. They will assign specific platform roles to managers, analysts, and
field staff. The IT personnel will register authorized Android devices through the web
portal. The system will restrict unauthorized mobile access automatically. The admin-
istrators will monitor account activity through immutable audit logs. These centralized
controls will reduce repeated permission changes significantly. The security team will in-
vestigate access problems much faster. The agencies will protect their sensitive campaign
data effectively.
Research Managers: The research managers translate client briefs into concrete
