Chapter 3
Software Requirements Specification
A Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the expected software behaviour,
system boundaries and quality constraints used to assess an implementation. It provides
developers, reviewers and intended users with a common basis for implementation and
acceptance testing. For the proposed system, the chapter specifies company access, ques-
tionnaires, campaigns, Android field collection, offline synchronization, media handling
and AI-assisted research. The requirements describe the behaviour to be delivered and
the conditions under which it is assessed. Detailed screen designs and database schemas
belong to the design stage [1].
Section 3.1 introduces the product scope and intended readership of the specification.
Section 3.2 defines ten functional requirements covering the company and respondent
workflows. Section 3.3 states quality constraints for response time, company data pro-
tection, offline records and Android resource use. Section 3.4 explains how technical
consultation, document analysis and frontend prototyping inform the requirements. Sec-
tion 3.5 presents the planned Scrum sprints and their increments.
3.1 Introduction
The proposed system connects a company web portal with an Android application
for on-ground market research. Field staff collect questionnaire responses and supporting
photographs or audio at assigned locations. The application saves records offline and
sends pending data to the server when connectivity is available. Server-side agents collect
information from approved public web pages and supplied documents, extract relevant
details, check the evidence and prepare research summaries. LangGraph coordinates
these tasks within defined permissions, while uncertain records remain subject to human
review. Outdoor advertising audits are one supported field-research use case.
Document Scope
The document specifies the functional requirements and quality constraints of the
product. The React portal supports company administration, questionnaires, campaigns
and reports. The Android application uses Kotlin and local SQLite storage for field collec-
tion. FastAPI services connect the clients with PostgreSQL records and the LangGraph
research workflow. GPS coordinates support assigned-site attendance checks and cam-
paign or location comparisons. The scope excludes physical billboard fabrication, hard-
ware maintenance and telecom infrastructure management. A separate Coding Agent

Audience
The specification gives the development team implementation requirements, product
owners and technical reviewers acceptance criteria, and operational users a description
of the supported tasks. The same requirements guide implementation checks, integration
testing and product acceptance.
 Development Team: The team uses the requirements to develop the React portal,
Kotlin Android client, FastAPI services, database records and coordinated AI tasks.
 Product Owners and Technical Reviewers: Reviewers compare the imple-
mentation and test results with the product requirements and defined scope.
 Testing Team: The team uses the requirements to check user actions, company
access, offline saving, synchronization and reported outputs.
 Company Research Staff: Company administrators, research managers, field
staff, analysts and reviewers check whether the described tasks match their assigned
responsibilities.
3.2 Functional Requirements
Functional requirements describe the operations that the proposed system performs,
the information it accepts and the outputs it produces. They provide a basis for imple-
mentation and functional testing [1]. Table 3.1 defines ten product workflows. Related
capabilities share records and supporting operations, including account access, media
links and collection identifiers.
Administrator means the administrator of an individual company workspace. Re-
searcher means an authorised research manager or analyst, Surveyor means a field staff
member, Reviewer means an assigned evidence reviewer, and Respondent means a survey
participant. Access to each workflow is restricted to the relevant company and assigned
role. Each requirement identifies a primary use case; related role actions and exception
paths form part of that workflow.
Table 3.1 links each functional requirement to its corresponding use case, allowing
reviewers to trace system behavior back to the needs of its users.

Software Quality Attributes
 Security: Company records shall be accessible only to authorised users of that
company. The application shall use HTTPS for data transferred to the backend.
Account protection shall rely on the standard safeguards of the selected, maintained
authentication component. Company administrators shall receive no access to other
company workspaces.
 Reliability: Saved Android responses shall remain available after an application
restart or an interrupted connection. Repeated synchronization attempts shall re-
tain collection identifiers and shall not create duplicate accepted submissions. Pend-
ing records shall remain on the device until the server confirms successful receipt.
 Usability: Questionnaires shall provide readable English and Urdu content where
configured, clear labels and specific validation messages. Submission states shall
distinguish saved, pending, synchronized and failed records. The survey interface
shall explain the purpose of collection and intended data use before participation.
Deletion of saved research records shall require confirmation.
 Maintainability: The implementation shall separate the web client, Android
client, backend and agent code through documented interfaces. The team shall
maintain version-controlled source code and reproducible setup instructions.
 Compatibility: The web portal and Android application shall exchange data
through the same documented API fields and formats. Question identifiers and

collection identifiers shall retain their meaning when records move between the

Performance Requirements
Routine web requests shall complete within three seconds for at least 95% of requests
in an evaluation with ten concurrent users and 5,000 stored survey responses. The target
excludes media transfers and external AI processing. The Android application shall save
a completed response locally within two seconds. On a stable connection of at least 10
Mbps, the app shall upload twenty queued responses without photographs or audio within
thirty seconds. It checks the transfer of responses saved while offline; media transfer time
depends on file size.
Safety Requirements
The Android application shall limit GPS capture, media recording and preliminary
AI processing to the collection or verification tasks that require them. It shall avoid
continuous background AI processing and repeated network retries when connectivity is
unavailable. These constraints limit unnecessary battery use and processing heat [2]. The
application shall present task and synchronization updates inside the app without audible
or vibrating alerts. Field staff shall enter data while stationary and review updates after
travelling.
Other Non-Functional Requirements
The web portal shall operate in the Chrome and Edge versions used for evaluation.
Deployment instructions shall identify the tested Android versions and required API
configuration. The Android application shall access the camera, microphone and location
only when the required device permissions are granted. Local and server records shall
remain subject to the company access restrictions stated in the specification.
3.4 Requirements Gathering Techniques Used
Requirements gathering identifies the needs, constraints and expectations that guide
implementation [1]. The product requirements baseline combines technical consultation,
analysis of relevant documents and review of the questionnaire prototype. These ac-
tivities clarify product boundaries, collection workflows and expected behaviour. The
documented consultation concerns technical review and scope clarification. Direct vali-
dation with external company representatives and field staff remains pending.
Technical consultation
Technical consultation gathers requirements through discussion of intended behaviour,
implementation constraints and delivery boundaries. The reviewer and development team

clarify needs and examine how the proposed functions fit together. The team records
the resulting decisions in the product requirements baseline and distinguishes technical
feedback from validation by operational users [1].
The development team used technical review feedback to clarify company access,
website questionnaires, offline Android collection and linked media records. The product
requirements baseline records these capabilities and the boundaries of collection, verifica-
tion, agent coordination and reporting. The review also clarified company responsibilities
and access restrictions.
The consultation clarified product boundaries, responsibilities and implementation
priorities. The documented feedback supports the technical baseline. Confirmation of
the complete workflow by operational company users and field staff remains pending and
is not presented as a completed consultation outcome.
Document analysis
Document analysis examines existing specifications, manuals, reports and other rel-
evant records to identify requirements, rules and constraints. The analyst records what
each source supports and identifies gaps that need clarification. The technique provides
an evidence base for requirements before the team commits to implementation [3].
The analysis used the product feature baseline, the existing-system review in Chapter
2, the SWEBOK requirements chapter and official ODK Collect documentation. The
feature baseline defined the delivery boundary. The ODK documentation provided a
concrete example of offline form filling, location capture and media collection [4]. The
review connected these documented capabilities with the corresponding workflows and
separated user actions from quality constraints.
The analysis connected the documented product capabilities with company access,
questionnaires, field collection, evidence review and reporting. It clarified the use of
location records for attendance checks and campaign comparisons.
Prototyping
Prototyping uses an early representation of the interface or workflow to clarify re-
quirements. Screens and working interactions make assumptions visible and help re-
viewers identify missing steps or confusing behaviour. The development team uses the
observations to refine the requirements before building the complete implementation [1].
The team developed a React questionnaire frontend with questionnaire management
and a respondent view. The prototype supported review of question design, response val-
idation, survey access and questionnaire deletion. The work clarified interface behaviour
before backend integration. It did not establish working database persistence or offline
operation in the Android application.

3.5 Time Frame
The planned implementation schedule contains four two-week sprints, matching the
Scrum process model in Chapter 2. Each sprint produces an increment for review. The
team begins with core records and questionnaires, then develops Android field collection,
AI workflows and reporting. Testing and documentation accompany implementation.
Sprint reviews guide adjustments to remaining tasks within the specified product scope
[5].
Table 3.2 divides implementation into four consecutive sprints and shows the planned
duration of each stage.

Sprint 2: Android collection and field verification 2 weeks (weeks 3–4)
Sprint 3: Multi-agent intelligence and media verification 2 weeks (weeks 5–6)
Sprint 4: Analytics, system evaluation and deployment preparation 2 weeks (weeks 7–8)
