1. Project Description
Professionals in outdoor advertising research pull data from scattered sources. Ana-
lysts process digital campaign logs, physical photographs, GPS coordinates, and paper
surveys every day. Managing these disconnected pieces across multiple cities creates a
massive administrative workload. Field staff currently run different software applications
for different tasks. This disjointed approach makes it difficult to match a specific photo-
graph to an exact street location. Office workers waste hours sorting loose files before they
even start evaluating market trends. The industry requires a single platform to secure
this evidence and drive the workflow directly from street capture to the final review.
The proposed system will operate as a central workspace to manage research from
initial planning to the final report. The architecture will avoid bulky, all-in-one software
designs. It will instead deploy a network of specialized AI agents to handle the workload.
Specific agents will take over distinct tasks, like collecting field metrics, running quality
checks, and drafting summaries. Each tool will finish its step and pass a clean data file
to the next agent in the sequence. A central database will anchor these moving parts
to track every source accurately. Whenever the automated tools spot messy data, the
system will flag those items for manual human review. The platform will establish a
reliable, end-to-end process built specifically for market analysis.
The new platform directly connects every research question to the raw field evidence
and the final reports. This traceable workflow provides teams with a dependable method
for market research. The unified architecture actively prevents common mistakes caused
by manual data management. Companies experience real operational improvements from
this direct connection. Field teams significantly reduce their administrative workloads.
Managers verify the submitted data much faster. Analysts publish the final reports
earlier.
The new system resolves immediate logistical bottlenecks. The platform also provides
a flexible base for future projects. Organizations easily plug in new data streams without
breaking the core pipeline. System administrators quickly assign different AI agents.
This structural design allows agencies to scale operations safely. Research teams maintain
complete visibility over their information. Executives base their final decisions on hard
facts.
2. Real World Problem
The out-of-home (OOH) advertising industry generates massive amounts of data as it
grows. This rapid expansion creates a strict demand for reliable evidence management.
The sector reports a record $ 9.46 billion in revenue for 2025. This figure marks nineteen
consecutive quarters of financial growth. Digital screens now account for 36.3% of that

total market. Brands spend heavily on these physical spaces. Advertising agencies require
consistent and transparent campaign measurement across all media types to justify these
brand investments. Geopath and the Out of Home Advertising Association of America
currently manage a new audience measurement initiative launched in March 2026 to
address these exact demands. The modern market simply needs stronger infrastructure
to process complex performance data and hold operators accountable.
Within the target organization, these challenges are reflected in a research workflow
in which survey responses, campaign details, field photographs, location information,
spreadsheet calculations, and web references are maintained through separate activities.
Researchers must manually reconcile these sources before comparing locations, time peri-
ods, or campaigns, while missing collection dates, duplicate responses, inconsistent nam-
ing, and unsupported information can complicate verification and analysis. The current
process also requires separate effort for planning research, collecting information, extract-
ing relevant content, checking data quality, verifying evidence, performing analysis, and
preparing reports. These activities correspond to distinct functions that can be handled
through specialized agents: a research planning agent can structure the research require-
ments; data collection agents can gather information from permitted sources; an infor-
mation extraction agent can organize collected content; quality and verification agents
can identify inconsistencies, duplicates, missing information, and unsupported claims;
and analysis and reporting agents can process validated evidence and produce traceable
findings.
If these gaps remain, the organization will face ongoing costs for manual data prepa-
ration, reconciliation, verification, and reporting. The likelihood of inconsistent or poorly
supported findings will also increase as campaign volumes grow. Broader data-quality
research will highlight the same concern: an IBM Institute for Business Value report pub-
lished in 2026 will show that 43% of chief operating officers identify data quality as their
main data priority, while more than one-quarter of organizations report annual losses
above US$ 5 million due to poor data quality. For the target organization, the workload
will provide a direct basis for estimating operational costs. A campaign covering 100
locations will require 50 staff-hours for manual reconciliation alone, based on 30 minutes
per location, before verification and reporting are included. Higher campaign volumes
will increase both labor demands and processing time.

8. Introduction
Market researchers collect and analyse information to answer business and market
questions. Researchers collect primary data directly for a specific study through ques-
tionnaire responses, field observations, photographs, GPS records, and interviews. Re-
searchers obtain secondary data from existing sources, including websites, reports, pub-
lic databases, market publications, and competitor records. In outdoor advertising, re-
searchers use both types of data to evaluate campaigns. Field workers record a billboard’s
location and condition during site visits, while questionnaires capture audience responses
and secondary sources provide market or competitor information. The system organizes
these different sources, verifies the collected information, and links each record to its
original source.
The proposed platform provides a coordinated workflow for market research across
multiple markets, campaigns, and locations. It manages campaigns and locations, re-
search questionnaires, and Android-based field collection. The system also handles ge-
ofenced attendance and visit verification, offline data storage, incremental synchroniza-
tion, media management, secondary data collection, quality checks, and cross-source ver-
ification. Specialized AI agents support research planning, data collection, information
extraction, verification, analysis, dashboard preparation, reporting, and coding tasks. Se-
lected AI models also run on mobile devices to perform preliminary analysis of images,
audio, and field data. An analytics dashboard visualizes and compares collected data.
Human reviewers remain involved in disputed or exceptional cases.
The text below previews the core industry challenges before setting up the boundaries
and main goals of the proposed system. Subsequent chapters will break down the overall
architecture, paying close attention to data syncing routines and AI agent pipelines. The
documentation will then list the required development tools, quality checks, performance
metrics, and the rollout timeline. To wrap up, a practical walkthrough will illustrate how
the platform handles raw field evidence through automated pipelines to produce the final
analytical reports.

10. Problem Statement
Market research teams face difficulties when they use separate tools for data collection,
verification, and analysis. Traditional software supports simple survey forms, but field
crews encounter problems when staff members record GPS coordinates, site photos, audio
notes, and research findings in different applications. Separate storage systems force staff
members to cross-reference multiple databases manually. Fragmented data systems also
weaken the connection between raw field evidence and final market conclusions.
Managing data across disconnected tools creates heavy manual workloads for both
field crews and office analysts. Researchers spend valuable hours matching site visits
against specific billboard locations. Field staff frequently lose survey entries when mobile
signals drop, leaving stray photos or audio files disconnected from their questionnaire
records. Whenever information goes missing or fails to match, teams must stop and fix
the errors manually before they can evaluate broader market trends. These recurring
administrative bottlenecks slow down data verification, dashboard updates, and final
client reporting.
Extended workloads and fragmented workflows will force organizations to rely heavily
on manual coordination and repeated validation steps as research volume increases. The
resulting heavy reliance will restrict the speed at which research can guide business deci-
sions and will prevent teams from building consistent, reusable processes across different
markets. Furthermore, this scattered approach will block companies from adopting auto-

mated artificial intelligence for field performance tracking, pitch evaluation, and real-time
evidence processing. Consequently, valuable market insights will remain trapped behind
slow, manual reviews.

12. Significance
The proposed system will make billboard research easier to trace from the initial
question to the final result. Researchers will be able to inspect which questionnaire
version produced a measure, which records were excluded, and which source supports
a statement. Field staff will have a defined route for submitting observations, while
analysts will work with campaign-linked evidence and consistent calculation rules. The
system will also provide documented calculations, source references, review records, and
controlled reporting for authorised users.
The proposed system will provide research teams with a coordinated workflow that
reduces repeated movement of information between questionnaires, spreadsheets, media
folders, evidence records, and reports. Companies will be able to manage campaign
information, field observations, audience responses, and supporting sources within defined
workflows. The system will also provide a basis for comparing manual, single-agent, and
multi-agent approaches through measured task performance. Its organisational value will
therefore be assessed through evidence rather than assumed time or cost savings.
The proposed system represents an evaluated implementation of specialised agents
within a constrained market research workflow. It examines how coordinated agents
support selected research tasks while preserving human review when evidence is disputed
or operations fail. Numerical correctness, source support, abstention, and recovery from
failures are evaluated separately so that generated explanations remain connected to
their supporting evidence. Consent, clear questionnaire wording, and visible sampling
limitations also support responsible research and clearer interpretation of findings.
13. Tools and Techniques
The proposed system uses web, mobile, backend, database, artificial intelligence, data
processing, evidence retrieval, and security technologies. The selected tools support re-
search planning, offline field collection, evidence management, data analysis, and report
generation. The selected technologies also support the implementation and evaluation of
the system’s functional modules effectively.
 Frontend Tools: React and TypeScript will provide the company portal, ques-
tionnaire website, interactive dashboards, and reporting interfaces. Flutter and
Dart will provide the Android field application, including offline questionnaires,
local submission queues, and synchronisation functions.
 Backend Tools: The backend services will use Python for application logic and
data processing. The backend services will use FastAPI for authenticated requests
and operations. The backend services will handle campaigns, questionnaires, sub-
missions, media, research runs, evidence, analysis, and reporting. The backend

--- PAGE 16 ---
services will use REST APIs to connect the web application and mobile applica-
tion.
 Database and Storage: The database system will use PostgreSQL to store com-
pany, campaign, billboard, questionnaire, submission, claim, source, and report
records. The mobile application will use SQLite to store queued field submissions
on Android devices. The storage system will use object storage for permitted pho-
tographs and documents. The evidence retrieval system will use pgvector to retrieve
authorised evidence.
 AI Techniques: LangGraph will coordinate bounded research agents through de-
fined states for planning, collection, extraction, quality review, verification, analysis,
and reporting. Retrieval-augmented generation will connect responses to authorised
evidence, while controlled prompts, tool permissions, execution limits, and human
review points will constrain agent operations.
 Data Processing: The data processing tools will include pandas and NumPy.
These tools will support data cleaning, transformation, aggregation, and indicator
calculations. The statistical analysis tools will include SciPy for selected statistical
procedures. The analytical functions will calculate campaign measures and provide
reference results for system evaluation.
 Evidence Retrieval: Approved public web sources and user-provided documents
will be processed through structured extraction and retrieval techniques. Source
identifiers, retrieval dates, passages, dataset versions, and claim-to-source links will
be retained so generated findings can be traced to supporting evidence.
 Security and Testing: The security controls will include role-based access con-
trol, company-level permissions, authenticated requests, app-scoped device identi-
fiers, audit records, and protected local storage. The development process will use
Docker, Git, code review, reproducible test fixtures, and automated test cases for
controlled development and system evaluation.
